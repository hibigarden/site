# the small blue

*draft three. the one where she stays.*

the butterfly came back on the last warm morning of october, which is not when small blues come back. ada wanted to tell someone, but there was no one in the garden at six except the drip timer clicking on in the shed, and the apple tree, which had dropped most of its apples on the path.

she found it on the lavender by the gate. it was smaller than her thumbnail, and the blue was only on the tops of its wings, so when it closed them it vanished into the grey of the dry flower heads. she counted to twenty while it was closed. then she counted to twenty again.

her mother had planted the lavender the year ada was born. it had been cut back hard every august since, and every spring it came back woody and stubborn and purple, the way her mother had, after the first operation and after the second.

"you're late," ada said to the butterfly, and felt foolish, and then didn't.

the timer clicked off. the water stopped running under the sunflowers. in the quiet she could hear the gate latch, still broken, knocking in the wind, and she thought that she would fix it today, and that she had thought that yesterday too.

the small blue opened its wings, and the morning got a little bluer.

she went to the shed for the screwdriver.
